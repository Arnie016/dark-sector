# Two clusters at matched physical resolution

We repeat the [two-cluster paired-map experiment](../two-cluster-common-beam/README.md) with both clusters smoothed to a nominal **135 kpc circular Gaussian FWHM**: 26.55 arcseconds for MACS J1752 (z = 0.366) and 36.27 arcseconds for PSZ2 G181 (z = 0.234), under flat H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3. This matches the nominal restoring beam in physical units, while observing noise, pixel scale, surface brightness, source extraction and transfer functions still differ. A 135 kpc target exceeds the input beams; kernel covariance and coverage were checked. The common physical radii are 300, 450 and 600 kpc, with 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 mJy/target-beam cuts. The illustrative estimator and catalog positions follow the prior analysis.

Across the three thresholds, signed T50 minus T50SUB pair separations in MACS are **+0.36 to +1.20 kpc** at 300 kpc radius, **−26.09 to −22.37 kpc** at 450 kpc, and **−60.76 to −55.48 kpc** at 600 kpc. PSZ2 differences are **−3.31 to −1.71 kpc**, **−2.32 to −1.50 kpc**, and **−284.96 to −254.76 kpc**, respectively. All 18 pair differences were independently recalculated from the 72 stored coordinate rows. These values persist at matched nominal physical resolution, but they are exploratory estimator sensitivities for related image products, not uncertainty intervals, physical separations to substitute into a clock, or evidence of a population-wide effect.

## PSZ2 location check

The largest positive T50 − T50SUB pixel inside the post-hoc southern 600 kpc circle lies 160.59 arcseconds from the published southern position, near the circle's approximately 161.19 arcsecond boundary. Its value is 32.11 mJy/target beam; pixels are correlated by the 36.27-arcsecond beam, and the selected peak is not a fitted source position. The [difference map](psz2-difference.png) shows the selected position, aperture and shared image structures. Merely locating a maximum does not prove that it causes the center shift; a controlled exclusion test is still needed.

A 30-arcsecond search in the [LoTSS DR2 raw radio catalog](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/659/A1) returns one source, `ILTJ093927.01+404350.3`, **20.54 arcseconds** from the difference peak. The catalog reports an extended multi-Gaussian source with fitted major/minor FWHM 36.95 × 21.77 arcseconds, including its 6-arcsecond survey beam. This is a positional association between related survey products; the separation, extended shape and post-hoc search do not establish source identity, cluster membership or a causal subtraction mechanism. No redshift is claimed.

## Reproduce

Run `python research/two-cluster-physical-beam/analyze.py /path/to/macs-maps /path/to/psz2-maps /path/to/rrsamp.tsv`. The script validates the four map hashes, source catalog hash, FITS units, kernel covariance and common finite coverage, and writes the summary, variants and plots. With the preserved raw VizieR response, run `python research/two-cluster-physical-beam/check_catalog.py /path/to/psz2-radio-cone.tsv`. The query, catalog identity and SHA-256 are recorded in the response and `catalog-summary.json`; the raw response is archived privately in Azure. NumPy, SciPy, Astropy and Matplotlib are required. Cite [Botteon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11720), [Shimwell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11733), VizieR DOI 10.26093/cds/vizier.36590001, and the [LoTSS cluster release](https://lofar-surveys.org/planck_dr2.html). The result is post-hoc and does not reproduce author masks, noise covariance, a reference clock, or a merger-age inference.

A subsequent [post-hoc exclusion experiment](../psz2-boundary-ablation/README.md) shows that the selected southern boundary region strongly influences one 600 kpc estimator configuration; it does not identify a physical source or quantify a published clock bias.

A separate [computational replay](../psz2-independent-reproduction/README.md) reproduces the PSZ2 position calculations using NumPy FFTs and explicit interpolation. The FITS WCS positions are FK5 J2000; some earlier comparisons read stored numeric positions as ICRS, introducing an approximately 0.021-arcsecond positional-frame systematic.
