# Illustrative uncertainty propagation for observed relic distance ratios

This is a numerical sensitivity exercise using the 11 redshifts and separation measurements transcribed from Table 2 of [Jee, Park & Lee, arXiv:2605.00093v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00093); see the adjacent `relic-gold-sample-data-check` for source and attribution. It is not the authors' inference, a remeasurement, posterior samples, or a constraint on dark-matter interactions.

For each system draw 200,000 bivariate normal realizations centered on the reported halo and shock separations, using reported symmetric uncertainties. Three correlations (-0.5, 0, +0.5) are hypothetical sensitivity settings; they are **not measured covariances**. Discard draws with either separation <=0 and summarize the 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles of the ratio. Compare with the first-order standard deviation including the same hypothetical correlation. A fixed seed and common normal draws across correlation settings make the calculation reproducible. These conditional distributions depend on the chosen Gaussian model and positivity truncation; they are not confidence or credible intervals for the actual systems.

For ZWCL1447.2+2619 (reported 0.36 +/- 0.15 Mpc halo separation and 2.01 +/- 0.02 Mpc shock separation), the zero-correlation model discards 0.814% of draws. The conditional 16th/50th/84th ratio percentiles are approximately 0.107/0.180/0.254, while the first-order standard deviation is 0.0746 around central ratio 0.179. An independent analytic normal-tail calculation gives approximately 0.82% negative halo draws and agrees with Monte Carlo within sampling tolerance. For this specific model and quantiles the first-order interval is numerically similar; the exercise does not establish whether the real measurement errors are Gaussian or independent.

The paper fits a forward model to the two separations with a diagonal likelihood and additional mass, phase and geometry priors; our ratio exercise does not reproduce or challenge its reported posterior. Actual covariance, shared systematics, nuisance parameters, selection and underlying lensing/radio data remain necessary for an inference. The three correlation scenarios are stress tests only.
