# Region and processing sensitivity of an illustrative radio-relic position estimator in MACS J1752.0+4440

**Exploratory working draft — not a submission-ready paper.** This is one post-hoc case study using related processed images. It does not measure merger-age bias, validate a published pipeline, identify a radio source, or constrain dark matter. Authors, uncertainty analysis, independent replication and journal selection remain open.

## Abstract

Relic-separation merger clocks depend on the definition of a relic position, while real radio fields contain extended structure and compact-source processing. We examine an illustrative bright-pixel center estimator on related LoTSS products for MACS J1752. Across nine catalog-centered radius and threshold choices, differences between paired T50 and T50SUB southern centers range from 0.01 to 15.90 arcseconds; signed north–south separation differences range from −15.80 to +0.20 arcseconds. In one post-hoc 90-arcsecond configuration, excluding a 20-arcsecond circle around the largest positive map difference reduces the paired southern-center difference from 15.20 to 0.12 arcseconds. A Keck catalog object lies 0.076 arcseconds from the LoTSS-associated optical position and 3.92 arcseconds from the difference peak. The radio beam, shared observational inputs, arbitrary masks and post-selection preclude causal source identification or a statistical uncertainty. A separate matched-beam comparison of two SUB maps gives a smaller signed separation range of −0.33 to +0.25 arcseconds for nine choices, but changes several processing characteristics and is not a controlled estimate of the subtraction effect. These diagnostics motivate a controlled, source-verified multi-cluster position study rather than a correction to published clocks.

## Context and question

[Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21632) model double relics in TNG-Cluster and TNG300 and report a time-since-collision relation using three-dimensional radio-emissivity-weighted centers. Their paper already discusses projection and merger complexity. [Jee et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00093) use a different model and observational sample to infer merger parameters and a conditional dark-matter constraint. We do not reproduce either analysis. The observational catalog and source products used here are described by [Botteon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11720); the released map names do not by themselves establish the full imaging or subtraction procedure.

Our concrete question is narrower: **under a stated exploratory position rule, how much do selected processed images of this same field change the estimated north–south separation, and which local image structures influence it?** This is not a test of whether the Lee simulation-center definition is reproduced by these observed positions.

## Data and method

We use the archived T50 and T50SUB maps, with verified file hashes, matching sampled celestial grids and nearly identical beam headers. The beam is approximately 14.73 × 10.64 arcseconds. For each published relic position, circular masks of 60, 90 and 120 arcseconds and brightness thresholds of 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 mJy/beam define nine paired settings. Within each mask and image, an illustrative flux-weighted center is calculated from the brightest 10% of eligible positive pixels. The pixels selected may differ between maps. These circles and thresholds are not the original authors' masks, a noise model or a pre-registered sampling plan. Full implementation, input hashes, configuration rows and details are in [the subtraction analysis](../macs1752-subtraction-sensitivity/README.md).

We use the previously selected positive difference maximum in a 120-arcsecond southern search area to define a post-hoc exclusion region, applied to both maps, and repeat one preexisting 90-arcsecond/0.2 mJy/beam setting. [The localization analysis](../macs1752-difference-localization/README.md) describes this intervention. A distinct comparison smooths T50SUB to the T100SUB beam and regrids it; it compares two separately processed products, not solely source subtraction ([method and checks](../macs1752-matched-centers/README.md)).

## Results

The southern center differences are 0.01–0.12 arcseconds for the 60-arcsecond masks and 14.76–15.90 arcseconds for the 90- and 120-arcsecond masks. Pair-separation changes across the nine choices span −15.80 to +0.20 arcseconds, using T50 minus T50SUB. For one post-hoc 90-arcsecond setting, masking the local difference peak in both maps changes the southern center difference from 15.20 to 0.12 arcseconds. This illustrates influence on this estimator, not a physical displacement or a calibrated systematic uncertainty.

The separate matched-beam SUB comparison gives paired separation differences of −0.33 to +0.25 arcseconds across its nine settings. The different comparison, beam matching, image products and masks prevent treating these two ranges as a controlled decomposition of all uncertainties. All measurements derive from related observations and correlated pixels.

The nominal compact-model image is spatially similar to the difference patch, but its FITS unit header and apparent normalization remain unresolved. In a spatial holdout from the same products, the unconvolved-component hypothesis has an all-finite-pixel residual RMS of 0.028 mJy/target beam versus 0.299 for a restored-image hypothesis. These are descriptive residuals, not noise uncertainties or evidence of significance; the hypotheses and products are related, and the precise model construction is unverified ([holdout analysis](../macs1752-model-holdout/README.md)).

A [Keck spectroscopic catalog](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/ApJS/240/39) reports z = 0.3708639 ± 0.0000217 for an optical object 0.076 arcseconds from the cataloged LoTSS optical position. Its location is 3.92 arcseconds from the difference peak; the radio position is 5.28 arcseconds from that peak. The beam does not resolve the association, and a close position and spectrum do not prove the object generates the radio emission or belongs to the cluster ([cross-match](../macs1752-keck-crossmatch/README.md), [overlay](../macs1752-radio-optical-overlay/README.md)).

## Interpretation and next test

The most defensible finding is a **conditional sensitivity of a chosen position estimator to region and image product in one field**. The local exclusion and shared image products make the case explanatory but not confirmatory. The distinct three-dimensional simulation and projected observational position definitions, unknown masks, covariance, event linkage and lensing likelihood prevent calculating a merger-age or SIDM effect from these results.

A follow-up capable of testing the broader question must freeze source-verified position definitions, acquire original regions and processing provenance, propagate beam/noise and astrometric uncertainties, and reproduce a published reference pipeline. Select additional clusters before inspecting their outcomes, include unsuccessful detections, and split at cluster level. Compare matched estimator variants on the same images and report directional separation differences with cluster-level uncertainty. Match independently verified simulation events and relics before evaluating held-out clock residuals. The full proposed protocol and open gates are in [the research protocol](../merger-paper-protocol/README.md). An independent analyst should rerun the pipeline before promotion to a manuscript.
