# PSZ2 G181: gamma-ray comparison and spatial scale

**Status: literature-based scale check, not a Fermi-LAT reanalysis.** [Deshpande, Manna & Desai (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11316) report a gamma-ray excess near PSZ2 G181 and prefer a radial Gaussian with sigma 0.4 degrees over a point source. The paper reports TS_ext=36.8 for that comparison. The authors' fit is their result, not an independent detection by this project. Its physical association with the cluster and emission mechanism remain to be established. Their fit at sigma 0.5 degrees was rejected after optimizer problems, and their disk-template likelihood increased with radius without a well-defined maximum; they describe neighboring-source and diffuse-background complications.

For context, [Ahn et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09067) report a total-system lensing R200c of 1.43 Mpc and approximately 2.7 Mpc radio-relic separation at z=0.234. Under the explicitly illustrative flat cosmology H0=70 km/s/Mpc, Omega_m=0.3, Tcmb=2.725 K, these correspond to approximately 0.107 and 0.201 degrees. A two-dimensional Gaussian with sigma=0.4 degrees has 68% containment radius 0.604 degrees, agreeing with the gamma paper; if placed at the cluster redshift, that angle corresponds to about 8.09 Mpc in projection. This conversion does not establish distance, physical size, membership, causal association, or a real edge. The lensing value is a spherical-overdensity radius, the radio value a separation, and the gamma value a containment radius of a smooth fitted template: they are not equivalent measurements. Their centers, errors, and cosmology conventions differ. No inference is made that gamma emission is resolved at either relic.

The preprint states Fermitools 2.5.1 and Fermipy 1.4.2 and describes `gtsrcmaps`/`gtlike`; it does not demonstrate that Fermipy `extension()` or `localize()` was used. [NASA's Fermi announcement](https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/) attributes a radial-Gaussian/disk source-map bug to **2.5.2**, not to the paper's 2.5.1. Separately, the [Fermipy 1.4.3 changelog](https://fermipy.readthedocs.io/en/1.4.3/changelog.html) reports a SourceMap caching bug affecting `extension()`/`localize()`, fixed upstream in Fermitools 2.5.3. The available descriptions do not establish that either issue affected this paper. Reproduction should record exact packages and methods and compare independently regenerated maps with an appropriate fixed release; do not infer a false retraction or invalid result from version numbers.

A defensible follow-up would obtain the authors' fit configuration, source maps and likelihood products, model the nearby source and diffuse backgrounds, test point/extended models with instrument-response and background systematics, and retain the energy-dependent PSF. NASA's [LAT data service](https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/access/index.html) supports bounded position, energy and time queries. The paper specifies a 5-degree radius, 1–300 GeV, MET 239587201–803865605; no event data, likelihood fit, full-sky download, software installation or paid compute was performed here. Acquiring exact derived products and independent validation remains open. No email is authorized without per-message approval.
