# A cross-paper check of four merger-clock summaries

This is a descriptive comparison of published numbers, not an independent validation, tension test, SIDM inference, or reproduction of either group's underlying analysis. The relevant studies share authors, simulation context and potentially overlapping observational inputs.

[Lee et al. (2026), ApJ 998 201](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21632), §IV.2, report the line TSC [Gyr] = 0.52 (d/R500c) - 0.24, fitted over d/R500c = 1–3.5. Their input for the fit is the three-dimensional separation between radio-emissivity-weighted relic centers; they also list approximate normalized separations for six observed clusters. [Jee, Park & Lee (2026), arXiv:2605.00093v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00093), Table 3, report model-dependent posterior merger phases for 11 observed clusters, using a fiducial phase prior of 0.4–1.0 Gyr. Four named clusters overlap with the Lee text. Transcribe Lee et al.'s approximate ratios and Jee et al.'s reported median and 1σ interval, and apply the Lee line as an illustrative calculation:

| Cluster | Lee ratio, approximate | Lee line, Gyr | Jee posterior median, Gyr |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ACT-CL J0102-4915 | 1.2 | 0.38 | 0.42 |
| CIZA J2242.8+5301 | 1.7 | 0.64 | 0.50 |
| ZWCL1447.2+2619 | 1.8 | 0.70 | 0.73 |
| PSZ2 G181.06+48.47 | 2.9 | 1.27 | 0.95 |

The last line-derived value is outside the Jee analysis's stated fiducial phase prior; this does **not** show a contradiction. Ratios are approximate; masses, radii, relic definitions, selection, projection, calibration scatter and assumptions may differ. Lee et al. report scatter that increases with normalized separation (around 0.3 Gyr at larger separations), whereas the displayed Jee intervals are conditional posterior summaries. They are not comparable as independent error bars. We have not independently confirmed identical underlying mass estimates or transformed the two models into a common likelihood. The four systems are not a representative sample and were chosen by overlap, not by a prespecified statistical design.

For PSZ2 G181.06+48.47, the [LoTSS DR2 catalog](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR-3?-source=J%2FA%2BA%2F660%2FA78%2Frrsamp) gives 2694 ± 39 kpc for the N/S relic pair, repeated on two rows. [Botteon et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11720) define their relic coordinates using flux-weighted positions of the brightest 10% of pixels; [Jee et al. Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00093) gives 2.70 ± 0.02 Mpc for shock separation. Central values agree to 6 kpc, but similar values do not prove matching extraction methods or independent uncertainty. Lee et al. explicitly discuss differences between projected shock edges and 3D emissivity-weighted centers, finding roughly 15% larger projected edge separations in their considered examples. Do not apply 15% as an established per-system correction. Our CSV reports the purely algebraic shift in the Lee line if its input were increased 15%, to illustrate sensitivity, not to infer a corrected age.

`sources.csv` transcribes the four rounded Lee text values and Jee Table 3 summaries. `python analyze.py` generates `comparison.csv`, `summary.json`, and the figure; `check_catalog.py /path/to/rrsamp.tsv` separately verifies the PSZ2 rows of the downloaded VizieR table. Raw survey data are retained in private Azure, not redistributed here; see `catalog-manifest.json` for the source, byte size and digest. Cite Botteon et al. and VizieR in any use and check the source reuse terms. Further work needs same-definition measurements, source-level provenance, radius and distance uncertainties, common selection, non-independent covariance, and a held-out baseline before physical conclusions or submission.
