# Two-body pericenter fit control

This extends the earlier straight-line control toward a physical dynamical model. We solve Newton's relative two-body equation for total point mass 10^14 solar masses, initialized at a known pericenter with tangential speed. We use pericenter distances 100, 300, and 1,000 kpc and speeds 3,000 and 5,000 km/s. These are idealized point-mass trajectories, not a TNG merger, hydrodynamic model, or an SIDM simulation. Gravity, but no extended halo, expansion, friction, mass evolution, tidal effects, gas or noise, is included.

For each orbit, solve forward and backward in physical coordinates using SciPy DOP853 and dense output at tight tolerances. Independently check the initial-state pericenter via the analytic Kepler energy/angular-momentum relation; all six pass. Over the sampled trajectory grids, fractional energy and angular-momentum drift are below 1e-7, and the minimum distance is at least the initialized value within numerical tolerance. This verifies the toy integration; it does not validate a cluster simulation. The dimensionful constants and error checks appear in `analyze.py`.

Sample 101 deterministic passage phases between snapshots 69 and 70 using the cosmological spacing of TNG snapshots 68–72, then apply the earlier unweighted five-point fit with the same acceptance criteria. These phase points are not a probability distribution. All fits pass. At 3,000 km/s, the median fitted-minus-true distances are approximately +105, +78, and +15 kpc for true pericenter distances 100, 300, and 1,000 kpc. At 5,000 km/s they are approximately +294, +212, and +65 kpc. The difference across these cases illustrates model and cadence sensitivity, not a real-catalog correction or uncertainty bound.

The [Lee et al. (2024) paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06340) describes five separation points near pericenter; this prototype's centered snapshot window and physical coordinate convention are assumptions, and the paper also discusses center-switch corrections that this control does not model. Do not claim an exact reproduction or novel physics from these results.

Reproduce `python analyze.py` with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib; the script records the source redshift-table digest and writes all phase-level cases, figure, and integration diagnostics. Validated with SciPy 1.17.1.
