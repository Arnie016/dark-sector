# Five-point pericenter prototype

[Lee et al. (2024), section 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06340), describes fitting a quadratic to five separation samples near first pericenter, and checking continuity to correct possible halo switching. This prototype asks how a plausible but not uniquely specified five-point implementation behaves against the current [TNG-Cluster merger catalog](https://www.tng-project.org/data/cluster/). It is **not an exact reproduction**: this code uses the five consecutively numbered snapshots centered on `Snap_coll`, rather than a validated reconstruction of the paper's nearest-pericenter points; uses nearest-image periodic separation; assumes the same flat matter-plus-Lambda cosmic-age conversion as earlier checks; and does not implement the paper's switching correction. Catalog construction and time conventions remain unverified.

Fit scalar separation versus cosmic time both in comoving ckpc/h and in proper kpc, without weighting. Accept only convex quadratics with vertex inside the five sampled times and nonnegative fitted separation. Of 2,083 events, 1,932 pass; 63 windows are outside the permitted snapshot range, 48 lack a unique complete window, 37 contain invalid positions, and three fail the fit conditions. These filters select event rows and may bias results. The 337 recorded `HaloID` labels among accepted rows are not verified unique present-day clusters.

Under the **comoving** fit, the median absolute difference from catalog `T_coll` is 0.0298 Gyr and the 90th percentile is 0.0650 Gyr; this assumes catalog `T_coll` is expressed on the same cosmic-age scale, which is not independently confirmed. The median absolute difference between the fitted comoving separation and `d_peri` is 72.3 ckpc/h, with 90th percentile 166.4 ckpc/h. The median shift between comoving- and proper-fit vertex times is 0.0083 Gyr, with 90th percentile 0.0231 Gyr. These are differences between methods under chosen assumptions, not error estimates or evidence of a catalog bug. A quadratic approximation to a sharp minimum can differ appreciably from an actual sampled minimum; conversion and selection choices matter.

The code verifies the input catalog SHA-256 and includes a synthetic irregular-grid quadratic recovery assertion; an independent least-squares calculation for a separate analytic quadratic also recovered its known minimum. Reproduce `python analyze.py /path/to/cluster_mergers.hdf5` with h5py and NumPy; the snapshot redshifts are taken from `../merger-collision-ensemble/snapshot_redshifts.json`. `fits.csv` provides per-event results and `summary.json` records filters and statistics. No raw dataset or credentials are included.

Next: establish catalog time and distance conventions, replicate the authors' exact window selection and halo-switch correction, validate branch IDs, test against analytic non-quadratic flybys and independent implementations, and compare with an identified published benchmark. Do not interpret differences as dark-matter effects or an observed merger result.

**Unit gate:** A later [distance audit](../pericenter-distance-unit-audit/README.md) found that many catalog `d_peri` values numerically match nearby proper-scaled separations despite the documentation labeling the field ckpc/h. Until independently resolved, the direct comoving-versus-catalog distance differences above may mix conventions and must not be interpreted as errors or used as a paper result.
