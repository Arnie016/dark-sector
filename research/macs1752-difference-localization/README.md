# MACS J1752: locating a radio-image difference

This follows the [paired T50/T50SUB experiment](../macs1752-subtraction-sensitivity/README.md). The official images have matching sampled celestial coordinates and near-identical beam headers. Subtracting their pixel values locally (T50 minus T50SUB), the largest positive difference within 120 arcseconds of the published southern catalog position occurs **84.47 arcseconds away**, with a pixel value of 26.55 mJy/beam. It falls outside the 60-arcsecond test circle but inside the larger circles. The image is a difference between correlated, separately processed products; neither the source identity nor the processing responsible is established. The color display clips at ±3 mJy/beam and therefore saturates the peak; the numeric peak is from the full array.

For the previous 90-arcsecond, 0.2 mJy/beam setting, the two independently selected bright-pixel centers differ by **15.20 arcseconds**. In a post-hoc diagnostic, exclude a 20-arcsecond circle around the difference maximum from **both** images before recomputing the same brightest-10% estimator: the center difference becomes **0.12 arcseconds**. This indicates that the identified patch has a strong influence on this particular estimator and mask. The exclusion was chosen after inspecting the images; it is not an author mask, a validated source-removal recipe, an uncertainty estimate, or evidence of a real relic displacement. Changing a mask also changes the number and identity of selected pixels, and the maps share observations.

The summary includes pixel sums solely as an internal diagnostic; these are **not integrated flux** because the pixels are correlated and no beam-area integration, uncertainty, or background model is applied. The original compact-source modeling, source identity, author masks, noise and association with merger shocks remain unverified. No correction to a published measurement, merger age or dark-matter conclusion follows.

Reproduce with `python research/macs1752-difference-localization/analyze.py /path/to/selected-maps` using NumPy, Astropy and matplotlib. It checks both source file hashes and matching grid/beam headers, hashes the earlier `variants.csv`, and records the input choices in `summary.json`. The original raw images are already in private Azure; the plot is a diagnostic with an intentionally saturated display. Cite the [survey cluster release](https://lofar-surveys.org/planck_dr2.html) and [Botteon et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.11720) when reusing data. No email was sent.
