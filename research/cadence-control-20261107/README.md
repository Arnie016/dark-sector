# Finite-difference cadence control for C14

A synthetic diagnostic using snapshot 30–99 redshifts from the TNG-Cluster event-5 history, not an estimate of the cadence error for that event. The values and cosmology are inherited from the previously checked C14 analysis; snapshot spacing is converted to flat matter-plus-Lambda cosmic time with h=0.6774 and Omega_m=0.3089, ignoring radiation. Median separation is 0.1573 Gyr. See [TNG-Cluster data documentation](https://www.tng-project.org/data/cluster/) and [field definitions](https://www.tng-project.org/data/docs/specifications/).

`python analyze.py` reads the pinned snapshot-redshift table and computes position-derived peculiar velocities for analytic sinusoidal comoving trajectories. The analytic derivative is known, and both velocities use the same scale factor and conversion. Oscillation parameters of 0.1, 0.3 and 1 Gyr are angular timescales (period 2 pi times the parameter), with a 1000 km/s comoving-derivative normalization multiplied by scale factor. These trajectories are illustrative and do not model a particular merger. Endpoints are excluded from summary statistics.

For the 0.1, 0.3 and 1 Gyr cases, respectively, median errors are 127.8, 16.4 and 1.53 km/s; the 90th percentiles are 303.9, 37.0 and 3.13 km/s. Inserting synthetic time midpoints reduces the corresponding median errors to 36.5, 4.16 and 0.382 km/s. Midpoints are model evaluations, not additional simulation snapshots; the midpoint experiment is an internal convergence check, not a reconstruction. The result demonstrates sensitivity to temporal variation and does not bound the real C14 discrepancy or establish a physical anomaly. Real position and velocity catalog fields represent different estimators.

Validated with NumPy 2.4.3 and Matplotlib 3.10.8. To take this toward a paper: match tracer definitions and branch IDs across real events; quantify sample selection and compare against a published baseline. Compare gas observables with their proper weighting, as discussed in [Ayromlou et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202348612). No SIDM or observed-cluster inference follows from this control.
