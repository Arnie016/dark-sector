# MACS J1752: separation and angular-scale provenance

This is a consistency and convention check, not a new relic measurement, correction to published results, or test of dark matter. The published [Botteon et al. catalog](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.11720) lists positions for the N and S relics. Their rounded coordinates are 6.3610 arcmin apart and the table reports a physical separation of 1941 ± 39 kpc. The coordinates and their source were independently identified in the [LoTSS cutout analysis](../macs1752-lotss-cutout/README.md).

[Finner et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.02226) quote redshift 0.365, flat H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc and Omega_m = 0.3, alongside a scale of 5.123 kpc/arcsec. Applying that quoted scale to the Botteon coordinates gives 1955 kpc. Their Table 5 reports 2323 kpc between radio-relic shock-front centers; Section V.2.3 states that the table distances are measured **parallel to their defined collision axis**. If the quoted scale was used, that axial projection corresponds to 7.557 arcmin along the axis. Botteon’s 6.361 arcmin is instead the full sky-plane separation of the published positions. These are different geometric measurements: the difference between their numerical angular values is not a measured centroid offset and the quantities cannot be used directly to reconstruct an age. Finner et al. describe their X-ray-defined collision axis as approximately 25 degrees from their relic-defined axis, but that does not establish the angle for the separate Botteon coordinates. The exact position selection, original map, uncertainty and actual conversion used for Table 5 remain unverified.

## Angular-scale check

Using the redshift and cosmological parameters stated by Finner et al., a flat matter-plus-cosmological-constant calculation with radiation set to zero gives **5.075 kpc/arcsec**, compared with their printed 5.123 (about 0.95% larger). Astropy and a separate numerical integration of the distance-redshift equation agree to numerical precision. At the Botteon coordinates this recomputed scale gives 1937 kpc; it changes the result by about 18 kpc relative to applying the printed scale, far less than the 382 kpc arithmetic difference between the two published central lengths, which use different projection conventions. The printed and calculated scales are not an explanation of why the papers differ. We do not know why the scale differs, whether additional assumptions were used, or which scale entered any published analysis, and do not claim an error in mass, separation, inference or article. Botteon's table lists z=0.366, distinct from the z=0.365 used in this particular check.

Run `python research/macs1752-separation-audit/analyze.py` from the repository root with Astropy; it reads and hashes the preceding cutout `summary.json`. The quantities are deterministic central-value conversions, not intervals or a posterior. The independent verifier uses SciPy quadrature of the flat-universe distance relation. The source products and definitions needed for a matched comparison remain open; no email was sent.
