# Provisional five-point fits versus pericenter catalog numbers

The [Lee et al. paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06340) describes a quadratic fit to five separation points near first pericenter. Our prior [prototype](../five-point-pericenter/README.md) used a particular centered five-snapshot window and separately fitted proper and comoving separations. It does not reproduce the authors' unverified window, unit, switching and interpolation conventions. The [official field table](https://www.tng-project.org/data/cluster/) labels `d_peri` ckpc/h; numerical evidence from a previous audit suggests a possible convention ambiguity that remains unresolved.

This check joins the previous prototype and sampled-minimum tables by event row and verifies their recorded catalog values agree. Of 1,932 joined events, 509 were among the 549 whose catalog number did not match any of three nearby proper-scaled separations. For these 509, our proper-coordinate five-point fitted numerical minimum falls within 0.01 of the catalog number in two rows; its median absolute numerical difference is 17.33. Our comoving fit matches zero within that tolerance, with median absolute numerical difference 82.50. The nearby proper-scaled sampled minimum has median absolute numerical difference 7.33. These comparisons may mix physical and comoving units; values are descriptive numbers, not physical residuals, goodness-of-fit statistics, or evidence that a release is wrong.

For comparison, 1,423 joined events were already selected because their nearby proper-scaled sampled minimum matches the catalog number. In that subset, the proper-coordinate five-point prototype matches within 0.01 in one row. This result does not rule out the published method: the released catalog may use different inputs or implementation, and our selection does not establish physical identities, independent clusters, or inclusion probabilities. The exact source implementation or author clarification is needed before making a paper claim.

Run `python analyze.py` with Python 3. Both input CSVs are produced by the adjacent analyses; their SHA-256 digests are recorded in `summary.json`, and the row-level joined comparisons are in `comparison.csv`. An independent standard-library check validated the joined and unmatched counts. These are same-simulation checks, not observational or SIDM constraints. No email has been sent.
