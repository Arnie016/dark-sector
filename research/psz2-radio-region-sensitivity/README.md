# Region-choice sensitivity for PSZ2 G181.06+48.47 radio positions

This is a deliberately bounded **sensitivity experiment**, not a new radio relic measurement, an uncertainty estimate, a reproduction of the original masks, or an independent validation. It uses the survey's processed 50 kpc taper, compact-source-subtracted radio FITS map already stored in Azure and the pinned VizieR catalog of published positions. [Botteon et al. (2022)](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2022/04/aa43020-21.pdf) define relic positions from flux-weighted positions of the brightest 10% of pixels within each relic; [Jones et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245102) describe manually defining relic regions and using emission above twice the image rms. We do not have those manual masks or a validated local noise map.

## Test and results

For each published N/S coordinate, we instead draw a **catalog-centered** circle of radius 90, 150 or 210 arcsec and keep pixels above an illustrative 0.1, 0.2 or 0.3 mJy/beam. We then compute the flux-weighted center of the brightest 10% of eligible pixels, independently for each of nine circle/cut pairs. These deliberately selected thresholds are **not** asserted to be 2σ, locally calibrated noise, detection thresholds, or a sample from a probability distribution. Our circular masks are not the authors' relic masks; unrelated emission, residual compact sources, excluded relic regions and correlated noise can affect them. Because the circles are seeded on the published coordinates, these estimates are not independent of the catalog. The radio beam is approximately 19.6 × 19.2 arcsec; pixels are 2 arcsec and strongly correlated.

Across these choices, the computed northern centers lie **19.8–40.4 arcsec** from the published northern coordinate; the southern centers lie **3.1–24.1 arcsec** from the published southern coordinate. The corresponding pair separations are **42–64 kpc larger** than the catalog-coordinate separation after converting both with z = 0.234 and flat H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3. These ranges are outcomes of this small, artificial design—not confidence limits, estimated bias of the original catalog, a detection of motion, or evidence that an author measurement is wrong. Changing a mask can change which pixels count as relic emission and which contaminants are included. The two altered separations are correlated because each is derived from the same image and published references.

For context only, if one substitutes those pair-separation changes into the Lee et al. (2026) line with R500c = 0.93 Mpc, the numerical output changes by roughly +0.024 to +0.036 Gyr. The observed brightness-center positions and the simulation's 3D emissivity-weighted centers are different observables; this substitution is **not** a physical merger age, correction, or comparison to a likelihood. Accurate physical comparisons require the original regions, noise/background maps, centroid covariance, consistent definitions, PSF/beam treatment, projection and a source-verified simulation baseline.

Run `python analyze.py /path/to/T50kpcSUB.fits /path/to/rrsamp.tsv` using NumPy, Astropy and matplotlib. The adjacent [manifest](../psz2-multiwavelength/manifest.json) pins both inputs. `variants.csv` records all 18 individual relic calculations, including pixel counts, centers and derived pair values; `summary.json` records the ranges and WCS warning. The script checks image dimensions, units, input hashes, positive pixel selection and circle bounds. A separate standard-library check verified the number of selected bright pixels and the reported range arithmetic; it is not an independent astronomical reduction.
