# Paper versus catalog collision-velocity definitions

This research note cross-checks two primary sources, both of which should be cited in any eventual study. Section 2.2 of [Lee et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06340) describes fitting a quadratic to five separation points around closest passage to infer pericenter distance and time, and describes collision velocity using the time derivative of separation. It also discusses halo-center switching and continuity checks. The current [TNG-Cluster catalog documentation](https://www.tng-project.org/data/cluster/) describes `V_coll` as the maximum relative velocity near first pericenter derived from `SubhaloVel`. The descriptions are not identical; differences in release version, processing, definitions, epoch, scalar versus vector velocity, or wording may matter. These sources alone do not establish that the paper and released catalog are inconsistent or that either is wrong.

## Diagnostic (not a paper reproduction)

For the 1,977 previously selected event rows, compute the 3-point centered finite difference of proper *scalar separation* at `Snap_coll`, with nearest-image wrapping and the same flat matter-plus-Lambda approximation used by C14. Proper-separation rate includes Hubble expansion. Compare the magnitude of that rate with the released `V_coll`, a nonnegative speed. This intentionally highlights distinct quantities: a scalar radial rate can be small near pericenter even when the full 3D relative speed is high. It is not the paper's five-point fit, does not reconstruct its collision velocity, does not independently validate the catalog or matched particle identities, and does not quantify an observational or physical effect.

The median absolute value of this three-point proper separation rate is 337.0 km/s; the median catalog `V_coll` is 2,101.2 km/s. The median of the per-row absolute differences is 1,673.7 km/s; the 90th percentile is 2,614.2 km/s. These are descriptive statistics for a filtered event sample, with repeated halos and no selection correction. They must not be interpreted as errors or comparisons between matched observables.

The released paper's procedure should be reproduced exactly before comparing against `V_coll`: verify whether separation is proper/comoving, infer the time and velocity estimation convention from the authors' method, implement the five-point fit, confirm branch and switching handling, and match a published benchmark with a validated halo identifier. Tests must include off-axis synthetic orbits where full speed and radial rate differ and cases with irregular snapshots. The target is a methods/selection study, not an SIDM constraint.

Reproduce `python analyze.py /path/to/cluster_mergers.hdf5` with NumPy and h5py. The script checks the corrected catalog digest, uses the pinned selection from `../merger-collision-ensemble` and the snapshot-redshift input there, and outputs row-level comparisons and summary. It is derived from one simulation, not an observed cluster sample.
