# Two-cluster comparison with a common angular beam

This exploratory follow-up compares paired T50/T50SUB maps in MACS J1752 and PSZ2 G181.06+48.47. It repeats a catalog-seeded, flux-weighted brightest-10% center rule after smoothing both fields' maps to a **25-arcsecond circular Gaussian beam**, converting nominal beam units and resampling each non-SUB map to its SUB grid. Catalog coordinates come from [Botteon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11720). All image pairs share observations; the smoothing and bilinear interpolation do not equalize their full transfer functions or reproduce the original imaging and source-subtraction pipeline.

We use radii of **300, 450 and 600 proper kpc**, with thresholds of 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 mJy per target beam. A flat H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3 cosmology and z = 0.366 / 0.234 provide the angular scales. All sampled circles have complete common finite-pixel coverage after the convolution validity check. Same angular beam is not same physical resolution: 25 arcseconds corresponds to about **127 kpc** in MACS and **93 kpc** in PSZ2. Different redshifts, morphology, observing noise, surface brightness and transfer functions remain relevant.

Across the three thresholds, signed T50 minus T50SUB north–south separations in MACS differ by **+0.11 to +0.45 kpc at 300 kpc**, **−31.27 to −29.18 kpc at 450 kpc**, and **−64.15 to −58.37 kpc at 600 kpc**. In PSZ2 the corresponding ranges are **−1.46 to +0.69 kpc**, **−3.37 to −1.98 kpc**, and **−265.66 to −232.35 kpc**. These are descriptive changes for particular, artificially chosen position rules—not confidence intervals or corrections to published separations. The PSZ2 large-aperture behavior was not visible in the earlier [60–120 arcsecond comparison](../psz2-paired-positions/README.md); its 600 kpc radius is about 161 arcseconds. The selected circles and fields were chosen after earlier results, and the affected source or processing mechanism has not been identified for PSZ2.

This is evidence that the **exploratory estimator is sensitive to aperture and map product in both fields** under this specific grid, not evidence of a population-wide bias, independent detections, an error in a published paper, or a merger-age or dark-matter effect. A meaningful follow-up requires matched *physical* resolution, source masks and processing history, noise/beam covariance, fixed selection and additional clusters. The simulation calibration uses different 3D relic definitions.

## Reproduce

Run `python research/two-cluster-common-beam/analyze.py /path/to/macs-maps /path/to/psz2-maps /path/to/rrsamp.tsv`. The four inputs and catalog are previously archived privately in Azure and pinned by SHA-256 in `summary.json`. The script checks FITS units, kernel positivity and discretized covariance, catalog identity and ≥99% common finite coverage. It writes `variants.csv`, `summary.json` and `comparison.png`; NumPy, SciPy, Astropy and Matplotlib are required. An independent coordinate calculation verified the angular center and pair differences for all 72 rows. No noise estimate, model fit or physical-clock calculation is claimed. Cite [Botteon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11720), the [VizieR catalog](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/660/A78), and the [LoTSS cluster release](https://lofar-surveys.org/planck_dr2.html).

A follow-up [physical-beam comparison](../two-cluster-physical-beam/README.md) retains the large PSZ2 aperture sensitivity after matching nominal physical resolution and locates a bright difference peak near the aperture boundary. Its physical source and causal role remain unverified.
