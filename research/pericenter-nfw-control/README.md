# Pericenter fitting in a static extended NFW potential

This control extends the previous Newtonian point-mass experiment with a stationary spherical [Navarro–Frenk–White profile](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9611107). The untruncated potential uses a scale radius of 250 kpc and mass 10^14 solar masses within a chosen reference radius of 1,000 kpc. The reference radius is **not** calibrated to the critical-density definition of R200 at the epoch. Its exterior mass continues according to the untruncated NFW profile. The orbit is a test particle in a fixed potential: there is no second extended halo, self-consistent mutual evolution, dynamical friction, expansion, gas, mass growth, or observational noise. Parameters are illustrative, not a fit to a TNG cluster.

Initialize trajectories at known pericenters of 100, 300, and 1,000 kpc with tangential speeds 3,000 and 5,000 km/s. Verify the speed exceeds local circular velocity, integrate both directions using DOP853, and check radial minimum, energy and angular momentum conservation. Energy drift is normalized by the larger of absolute initial specific energy and GM/reference radius; maximum normalized energy and relative angular momentum drift are below 1e-7. An independent numerical integral checked enclosed-mass normalization, and finite differences checked that the potential gradient matches the acceleration for all three radii. These checks validate the toy calculations, not the physical applicability of a static spherical halo.

Sample five times with TNG snapshots 68–72 spacing and place the known pericenter at 101 deterministic phases between snapshots 69 and 70. Fit the same provisional unweighted quadratic used previously. For the 3,000 km/s cases, the median fitted-minus-true pericenter distances are +173, +88 and +16 kpc respectively. The earlier point-mass control with the same mass parameter and initial radius/speed gave +105, +78 and +15 kpc. They are distinct potentials; the phase grid is not a statistical distribution, and neither test bounds or corrects real TNG outcomes.

[Lee et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06340) describe five points near pericenter but their exact window, units and center-switch treatment are not reproduced here. Code: `python analyze.py` with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib. The pinned snapshot redshift digest, per-phase results, fit acceptance, and numerical checks are saved.
