# MACS J1752: merger-age and radio-structure cross-check

**Status: source comparison, not an independent age estimate or SIDM constraint.** MACS J1752.0+4440 is a useful stress-test candidate for the merger-clock project because the same object appears in multiple analyses, but their data and authors overlap and their models do not estimate age in identical ways.

- [Finner et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.02226), summary, report 0.9 ± 0.2 Gyr for their MCMAC analysis, 0.2–0.5 Gyr from cosmological analogs, and 0.21–0.35 Gyr using radio-relic methods. These are different model-conditioned estimates within the same study, not independent measurements. The main text also reports 0.9 ± 0.1 Gyr for a specific MCMAC outgoing solution; we retain the summary value above and do not silently harmonize the two. Their paper explicitly discusses an expected bias in MCMAC timing.
- [Jee et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00093), Table 3, report 0.44 +0.03/−0.02 Gyr under their forward model and stated fiducial 0.4–1.0 Gyr phase prior. They identify MACS J1752 as their most informative individual gold-sample system; their jackknife changes the reported joint 68% upper limit from 0.22 to 0.38 cm²/g when it is removed. This does not show that the older MCMAC estimate invalidates their result; the authors report a range of prior sensitivity tests. A joint likelihood and common definitions would be needed to assess effects quantitatively.
- [Della Chiesa et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02410) present high-resolution uGMRT, JVLA and LOFAR radio analysis. Their northeastern relic has a bright bar and multiple spectral/brightness features, and the authors discuss multiple shock surfaces, reacceleration and projection as possible explanations. They cite an approximately 0.9 ± 0.1 Gyr earlier estimate; that is an attribution to previous work, not an independent new age. The observed morphology is a reason to examine relic-position definitions, not proof of a particular selection bias in either merger-age model.

The [Lee et al. (2026) simulation clock](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21632) uses three-dimensional radio-emissivity-weighted centers; observed edge, bright-bar and projected positions cannot be inserted as though they were that quantity. Reanalysis should obtain original radio maps, beam/noise and masks, weak-lensing measurements and their joint posterior, and each model's definitions and code. First reproduce each source's reference analysis; then compare documented position conventions in a cluster-level held-out sample. One complex cluster does not establish population-wide bias.

This note records reported values verbatim in meaning; no data were downloaded or refitted. The paper request to Dr. Lee is still unsent and requires the user's explicit per-email approval.
