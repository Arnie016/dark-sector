# Correcting a present-day merger selection

The [TNG-Cluster documentation](https://www.tng-project.org/data/cluster/) defines `Snap_full_coll` as the closest full snapshot after first pericenter and `TSC_full` as the time since collision at that full snapshot. An earlier local note called the selection `Snap_full_coll == 99`, `0 <= TSC_full <= 1 Gyr`, and `Mass_ratio >= 0.1` a complete present-day recent-merger selection. That label was too strong: it conditions on the first full snapshot after pericenter being snapshot 99, rather than directly measuring time since collision at z=0.

An exploratory alternative calculates `age(z=0) - T_coll` using the same flat matter-plus-Lambda model as C14 (h=0.6774, Omega_m=0.3089, Omega_Lambda=0.6911; age 13.8027 Gyr), and selects values in [0,1] Gyr with `Mass_ratio >= 0.1`. On the pinned corrected catalog, the old criterion selects 103 event rows; the alternative selects 107, including all 103 old rows and four additional rows. The 71 and 74 distinct `HaloID` values respectively are recorded halo identifiers at merger, not proven independent z=0 cluster identities.

This comparison is **provisional**: the official documentation describes `T_coll` as the time of first pericenter, but does not specify enough in the retrieved field description to establish the precise age convention. A consistency check across all 2,083 rows comparing our cosmological age at `Snap_full_coll` minus `T_coll` to `TSC_full` has median absolute residual 0.0051 Gyr, 90th percentile 0.0818 Gyr and maximum 0.3082 Gyr. Thus the simple time convention is plausible for many rows but does not reproduce the catalog timing to precision for all rows. Boundary membership may change, and the 107 rows must not be reported as a validated present-day sample. Inspect the source paper/catalog construction and verify snapshot times and branch IDs before drawing population conclusions.

The [Lee et al. paper](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202348194) describes a massive present-day system labeled Halo 3 with a prior collision roughly 0.87 Gyr ago. We have not established a verified mapping between that published halo identifier and the merger catalog's per-event `HaloID`; matching numeric labels alone is insufficient. Do not claim a reproduction of that example until its identity and history are validated.

Run `python analyze.py /path/to/cluster_mergers.hdf5` with h5py and NumPy. The script checks the catalog digest; `summary.json` records exact selected rows. This is an internal selection audit, not a measurement of a merger rate or new physics.
