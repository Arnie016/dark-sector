# Pericenter values and nearby sampled minima

The [previous audit](../pericenter-distance-unit-audit/README.md) found that many numerical `d_peri` values match a nearby separation after converting documented comoving positions using a/h. The [official catalog documentation](https://www.tng-project.org/data/cluster/) labels `d_peri` ckpc/h, while [Lee et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06340) describes a five-point fit near pericenter. The released field's exact construction and unit convention are not established by this analysis; do not relabel the field or treat this as an independently verified catalog defect.

For the same 1,977 previously selected rows with valid histories at `Snap_coll-1`, `Snap_coll` and `Snap_coll+1`, calculate their proper-scaled separations and identify the minimum of those three values. With an absolute numeric tolerance of 0.01, 1,428 rows match one nearby proper-scaled value, and all 1,428 also match the minimum among those three. The closest numerical matches occur at the central snapshot for 1,415 events, one earlier for four, and one later for nine. The other 549 do not match any of the three. These are within-catalog algebraic comparisons, not independent replication. The tolerance is not an observational error, and this selection is not an unbiased sample of clusters.

This pattern is consistent with—but does not prove—the numerical use of a nearby sampled minimum in part of the release. It cannot distinguish release changes, unit conventions, interpolation, center-switch corrections or selection details. The paper's five-point method and current `d_peri` documentation must not be assumed to be identical based on these values. Do not derive a correction factor or use previous direct comoving-versus-field discrepancies as an error estimate without independent verification.

An independent standard-library count of `comparison.csv` matched the summary. Results are simulated event records and do not constitute an SIDM or observational conclusion.
