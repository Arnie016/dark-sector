## Physical question
Can a dark-sector sidereal-day (23h56m) signal be distinguished from a laboratory background repeating each 24h solar day with intermittent observing? How does the answer change if the background is nonstationary between seasons?
### One explanatory AHA
A four-minute daily clock difference builds up **across elapsed calendar time**, not just live observing hours. Two 30-day campaigns separated by about six months can retain much more discriminatory information than 60 consecutive days with identical live hours, **only if the solar daily background has a shared amplitude/phase across campaigns**. If each campaign has independent solar harmonic nuisance coefficients, the advantage disappears. This is a conditional observability statement, not a DM discovery.
## Mathematical tool: nuisance projection and Fisher eigenvalues
For rate observations at times `t_i` (solar days) define sidereal signal columns `S=[cos(omega_sid*t),sin(omega_sid*t)]`. The two coefficients encode an unknown amplitude and phase. Background matrix `B` has one free offset for each observing campaign and solar harmonics `cos(omega_sol*t),sin(omega_sol*t)`. A flexible nuisance model gives each campaign its own solar-harmonic coefficients. Frequencies: `omega_sol=2pi rad/day`; `omega_sid=2pi(1+1/365.2422) rad/day`. Given equal independent Gaussian rate residuals, profiling `B` retains `S^T(I-P_B)S/sigma²` information, where `P_B=B(B^TB)^(-1)B^T` (when full rank). Remove campaign offsets from `S`, orthonormalize to `Q_s`; orthonormalize nuisance columns to `Q_B`. Eigenvalues of `K=Q_s^T(I-Q_BQ_B^T)Q_s` are retained relative-information fractions for the two sidereal coefficient directions. Smallest eigenvalue is worst phase, with conditional standard deviation multiplier `1/sqrt(lambda_min)`. **Neither eigenvalue is a modulation amplitude, detection probability or sensitivity bound.** When sidereal and solar frequencies are identical, the residual vanishes exactly (limiting control).

## Competitor, falsifier and next gate
Strongest null: solar-time thermal/readout effects combined with real uptime gaps can imitate the assumed harmonic. In the actual DAMIC-M 2024-25 exposure, temperature was correlated with single-electron backgrounds, CCD channels changed availability and data were segmented. The comparison must therefore model temperature and segment offsets, not merely use a visually convincing folded-rate plot. Stop before any particle constraint until actual per-exposure masks, event counts or rate uncertainties, temperature and a physical detector response are available. Test whether a sidereal component remains identifiable once background coefficients vary as demanded by controls.
